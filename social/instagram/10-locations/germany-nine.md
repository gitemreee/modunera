# Place tags — Germany, the launch nine

Nine regions chosen for camping and holiday-home density rather than for population. A tiny house is bought by someone who already thinks about a plot, a pitch or a second home, and in Germany those people are concentrated in the coastal islands, the lake districts and the low mountain ranges — not in the cities. Each place has a real page under /standorte/, so a post that carries the tag has somewhere on the site to land.

## The rule for hashtags

Five per post. Three for the place, two for intent. The branded tag is gone: clicking #modunera from a MODUNERA post returns the reader to MODUNERA posts, which is a link back to where they already are. A branded tag earns its slot only when other people use it — customers posting their own house — and nobody does yet, so it collected nothing while costing a fifth of the budget. It belongs in the bio instead, where it accumulates without spending a post. The generic category tags are out for a different reason: #tinyhouse and #tinyhousedeutschland are the most contested terms in the market and a new account does not rank in either, while #vulkaneifel is a small pool of people already standing where a tiny house would go.

## Before you tag

An Instagram place tag is read by some people as 'this is where the picture was taken'. None of these photographs were taken in Germany — production is in Türkiye. Posts 1, 6 and 9 say so in the caption, so the tag reads as market. Post 5 does not, and it is the one that asserts 'a completed unit, not a visualisation' most directly; either skip the tag there or accept that it reads as a location claim. Recommended: tag it, because the account bio and every neighbouring post state the origin — but it is a decision, not an oversight.

## Why each one

**9 — Ostseebad Binz, Rügen.** Germany's largest island. Everything on it arrived over a bridge or on a ferry, which is exactly the subject of the transport post.

**8 — Bodenmais.** Beside the Bavarian Forest National Park — the largest contiguous woodland in central Europe. A fire-safety message belongs where the forest is.

**7 — Winterberg.** Sauerland is the holiday region for the Ruhr, which is the largest population block in the country. A workmanship post in front of the biggest domestic audience.

**6 — Titisee-Neustadt.** Black Forest, and the corner of Germany closest to Switzerland — the right place for the post about five countries and one route.

**5 — Waren (Müritz).** The largest inland lake district in Germany, and the centre of the grid deserves the largest market. Evening light on water is what people come here for.

**4 — Bispingen.** The Heide sits between Hamburg and Hannover and is one of the densest holiday-park areas in the north.

**3 — Gerolstein.** Vulkaneifel is a nature park with heavy camping traffic and cheap land by western German standards. A structural post suits a region people go to for the ground itself.

**2 — Prien am Chiemsee.** The Chiemsee shore is dense with campsites and holiday plots, and Bavaria is the strongest region for second homes.

**1 — Fehmarn.** Baltic island with one of the highest campsite densities in Germany, and a long-standing holiday-home market. The opening post gets the strongest place.
