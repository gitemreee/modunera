Feasibility

What actually decides it.

These four points decide most projects in this field. Settled early they cost nothing; settled late they cost the schedule.

01

Weight nobody notices

In a tiny house every kilo lands on the axle load. Furniture is where weight is easiest to add and hardest to see, which is why the material and construction of the built-ins are planned together with the total weight and not after it.

02

The space between the furniture

On a small footprint it is not the furniture area but the gap between it that decides whether the room is usable. One more centimetre of worktop can be one less circulation route.

03

The sequence is binding

Built-ins go in before the wall closes, because power, water and ventilation run behind them. A late change to the kitchen means opening the wall again — the most expensive change request in the whole project.

04

Material does not behave the way it photographs

What looks identical in a picture behaves very differently at the sink edge and after five years of hotel use. Selection follows wear and repairability, not the sample board.

Scope

What is included, and what is not.

The second column is the important one. Foundation, connections and permits are the items a quotation does not contain and a project needs anyway.

In scope

  • Design, manufacture, finishes and hardware
  • Installation in a unit supplied by us
  • Cut-outs and service preparation for the agreed appliances

Not included

  • Appliances unless explicitly quoted
  • Connection to water, wastewater and power on site
  • Installation in third-party buildings unless contracted
