Feasibility

What actually decides it.

These four points decide most projects in this field. Settled early they cost nothing; settled late they cost the schedule.

01

Zoning, before anything else

Whether the site may be built on and whether it may be lived on are two different questions — and the second is the most common reason a project fails. In Danish sommerhusområder and Dutch recreation zones, year-round living is restricted in principle, including where the most attractive plots are.

02

The foundation is part of the schedule

Strip, pad or raft is decided by the ground. What matters more for the date: the foundation has to be finished, cured and dimensionally correct before the unit arrives. A delivery date without a finished foundation is not a date.

03

Beyond transport width it arrives in sections

Anything wider than may be carried on the road is delivered in sections and joined on site. That is normal and planned for — but it changes the crane, the assembly time and the joint that has to stay weathertight afterwards.

04

Services are rarely already there

On resort and campsite land, water, wastewater and power are often not where the unit is going. Checking that after delivery leaves a finished building with nothing to connect it to.

Scope

What is included, and what is not.

The second column is the important one. Foundation, connections and permits are the items a quotation does not contain and a project needs anyway.

In scope

  • Unit finished through to the connection point
  • Interior fit-out and furniture where agreed
  • Factory inspection and documentation

Not included

  • Foundation, site services and connections
  • Permits and certificates in the destination country
  • Landscaping, decking and access unless contracted
