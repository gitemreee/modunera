Scale

From one module to an entire destination.

The same manufactured module is the starting point whether a project needs one unit or a hundred. What changes with scale is everything around it: site planning, infrastructure, production batches, transport legs and installation sequence — and that is what a project brief is for.

Project development

Complete project thinking

Eight stages from brief to handover, with who takes part and what each stage does not promise. Read how we work →

Discovery and project brief

Country, land, climate, purpose, capacity, number of units, budget band, target date and planning stage.

Feasibility and scope review

Local regulations and permits, transport access, foundations and site infrastructure, technical requirements, the need for local consultants.

Concept design and master planning

Site layout, unit types, access routes, shared facilities, repeatable modules — schematic drawings for decisions.

Engineering and technical specification

Structural, electrical, mechanical, thermal and acoustic performance, fire safety, accessibility, local norms — per discipline.

Commercial proposal and contracting

Unit and service breakdown, delivery terms (Incoterms), responsibilities, estimated programme, exclusions, currency and payment stages.

Production and quality gates

Production planning in batches, in-process checks, factory acceptance and reporting as agreed in the contract.

International logistics and site preparation

Road, Ro-Ro, sea container or oversized freight; port, customs, crane, foundations, access, utilities ready for the delivery sequence.

Installation, handover and aftercare

Installation responsibilities, test and handover documents, warranty, maintenance and service scope — local partner and manufacturer roles separated.
